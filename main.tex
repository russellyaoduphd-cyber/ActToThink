\section{Introduction}
LLMs' reasoning has experienced huge improvements these days, including Chain of Thought (J. Wei, X. Wang, D. Schuurmans, et al., “Chain-of-thought prompting elicits reasoning in large language models,”), ReAct(https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629), and other frameworks. These advances have significantly enhanced the high-level reasoning abilities of agents. Incorporating these advanced reasoning and language comprehension capabilities with embodied intelligence to formulate precise and efficient action plans based on natural language instructions intrigues a lot of attention(some reference). However, a prominent problem emerges: incorporating LLM-based reasoning into embodied robotic systems introduces a critical challenge. Highlevel reasoning is computationally expensive and often incurs
substantial latency, making it impractical to invoke indiscriminately during task execution (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.04334). In real-world robotic
deployments, excessive reliance on LLM-based reasoning
can delay action execution and disrupt interaction with the environment, while insufficient reasoning frequently leads to
incorrect decisions, unsafe behaviors, or task failure. In other words, due to resources limitation, "When to Think" needs to be addressed better.
